What is Vow.id?

Vow.id is a web app that you log in to with your wallet. You prove real purchases, for example at Amazon, and are proposed Member Rewards (MR) for them. Approved MR can be converted into v$ by contributing VOW; v$ are digital discount vouchers.

€0is what signing up costs. You only pay a small BNB network fee.
0.002 BNBis the minimum that must be in your wallet (BNB Smart Chain).
Foreveris how long wallet, referrer and handle are fixed. None of them can be changed later.
Not cashbackMR are not money. To redeem them you add VOW worth half the market value of a v$.

What works today: sign-up, the receipt address, the bank link for Premium and the wallet scan. Purchases are recorded and shown as MR proposals.

What is not running yet: the review of proposals by independent verifiers. According to a notice from Vow.id, it is waiting for a smart contract that is to be deployed “shortly”. Until then, MR stay on “Pending” and cannot be redeemed.

Vow.id is labelled as a beta. Not every receipt and not every bank is recognised yet.

What do I get out of it?

An honest breakdown, split into what you have straight away and what only time will show.

Straight away, if you work through this guide

  • A secured wallet of your own and the knowledge of how to avoid scams and costly beginner mistakes. That helps you with anything to do with crypto.
  • A finished Vow.id with a deliberately chosen wallet, the right referrer and your own handle.
  • From now on your purchases are recorded and proposed as Member Rewards.

Long term: saving on regular spending

  • For purchases you make anyway, you collect MR: up to 10% in the free account, up to 40% with Premium at selected providers and up to 100% on energy bills. The rates can change at any time. What 100% means in practice is explained in the questions under “How does this pay for itself?”.
  • You convert approved MR into v$ and use them as a discount, today at Vow.Market, later also for hotel bookings and vouchers according to announcements.
  • Worked example with the prices of 22 November 2026: a $100 purchase at 10% gives 10 MR. Redeeming costs VOW worth about $1. The 10 v$ count as a $10 discount at Vow.Market or are worth about $2 on an exchange.
  • The condition: redeeming has to start, and you have to find something at the merchants that accept v$ that you would buy at that price.

Long term: referring others

  • For every person who signs up through your link and proves their first verified purchase, an MR proposal can be created for you.
  • You can receive MR on the ongoing purchases of your network. The guide value is 0.5% of the purchase value, spread across ten levels, so one twentieth of a percent per level.
  • Conditions apply: for levels 1 to 3 you must lock up VOW worth at least $500 for 1,000 days, for levels 4 to 10 exactly $10,000. You must also work as a verifier yourself every 30 days. These roles have not been activated yet.
  • For a sense of scale: ten people you referred directly, who together spend $5,000 a month at listed providers, bring you around 2.5 MR a month on the first level.

To put this in context: MR are not money and not income. They are discount rights that first have to be approved and then redeemed with VOW. Vow.id explicitly guarantees no income. Referring others is a possible extra for people with a large network who use the system themselves, and no substitute for a salary.

Part 1

Set up and secure a wallet

A wallet is an app you use to identify yourself to services like Vow.id and to manage crypto assets. It belongs to you alone. There is no “forgot password”: the only spare key is your seed phrase.

  1. Choose a wallet app

    You need a wallet that can handle the BNB Smart Chain. Trust Wallet is used as the example here because it is offered directly in Vow.id's wallet selection. MetaMask or TokenPocket work just the same.

    Choose carefully: your Vow.id stays tied to this wallet forever.

  2. Install the app from the right source

    Go to the provider's official website and follow the link from there to the App Store or Play Store. Do not search for the app freely in the store and do not click on adverts. There are fake wallet apps that steal funds.

  3. Create a new wallet

    Open the app and choose “Create new wallet”. Set a lock code for the app. The app now shows you your seed phrase: 12 English words in a fixed order.

  4. Write down the seed phrase

    Write the 12 words by hand on paper, numbered and in the right order. Check every word. The app will then ask you for them once.

    The right wayOn paper, two copies, in two separate safe places. For example in a safe at home and in a sealed envelope with someone you trust.
    Never thisScreenshot, photo, notes app, email to yourself, cloud, WhatsApp, password manager on the same phone. And never dictate or show them to anyone.

    Whoever has the words has the wallet. Whoever loses them, and the phone as well, has nothing left.

Part 2

Get some BNB into the wallet

Vow.id runs on the BNB Smart Chain. Every transaction there costs a small fee in BNB. To sign up, at least 0.002 BNB must be in your wallet, a very small amount. Take a little more so it also covers later steps.

  1. Find your address

    In the wallet, tap “Receive” and choose BNB Smart Chain. The app shows a long address beginning with 0x. That is your public address. You may pass it on; it is like an account number. Always copy it with the copy button and never type it out.

  2. Get BNB

    There are two usual ways:

    • Buy in the wallet. Many wallets have a “Buy” button. Behind it are payment services that accept card or bank transfer and usually require a short identity check. Choose BNB on the BNB Smart Chain. Compare the fee shown; it is often high for small amounts.
    • Have it sent to you. Someone who already has BNB sends a small amount to your address. Or you buy on a crypto exchange and withdraw from there.

    Watch the network: When sending or withdrawing, “BNB Smart Chain” must be selected, often also called “BSC” or “BEP20”. A wrong network is the most common beginner mistake. The money then does not arrive and is usually lost.

  3. Check that it has arrived

    After a few minutes the amount should appear in your wallet under BNB (BNB Smart Chain). Only continue once you see it there. With larger sums, always send a small test amount first.

Part 4

The first purchase

Now you connect Vow.id to a shop where you buy anyway. Start with a single provider.

  1. Choose providers

    Open Member Rewards → Checklist and choose your country. Mark only the providers you really use. The list is your reminder list; you add forgotten providers later via “Edit services”.

  2. Enable receipts

    Open “Receipts & MR rights”. Switch on “Collect MR rights”, tick “I have read and agree” and confirm. “Shopping insights” is optional and does nothing for your rewards.

  3. Change your email at one shop and buy something

    Amazon is the easiest: under “Login & security” there, enter your name@vow.id as the account email. The confirmation code is forwarded to your normal inbox. You keep reading your mail there.

    Then shop as normal. The purchase appears under Member Rewards → MR, at first with the status “Pending”.

    Do not change: Google (and with it YouTube), Apple, Microsoft and your bank. The @vow.id address only accepts mail from recognised shops. With other accounts, security emails can get lost.

Part 6

Later: exchanging euros for crypto

For signing up and collecting rewards you need nothing apart from that little bit of BNB. Buying crypto only becomes a topic when you want to pay for Premium with USDC or USDT or redeem approved rewards with VOW.

  1. Understand the principle before you buy

    • Buying: Through your wallet's buy function or a purchase service you pay euros and receive, for example, USDT or USDC at your address. Expect an identity check and fees.
    • Swapping: You get VOW by swapping USDT, USDC or BNB for VOW on the BNB Smart Chain through an exchange such as PancakeSwap. Every swap costs a fee, and the price fluctuates.
    • Sort out the way back: Before buying, find out how you get back into euros and what that costs. It is not equally easy with every service and every token.
    • Start small: Make your first purchase with an amount you would not mind losing. Crypto assets can fall sharply.

    Which routes members use for this is described further down in the frequently asked questions under “Where do I get VOW for redeeming?”.

Frequently asked questions

When something gets stuck

Understanding approvals

Sooner or later an app will ask you for an “approval”. That is something different from the free signature at login. Vow.id explains the terms in its security area like this:

  • Token approval: You allow another address or a smart contract to spend a certain amount of a token from your wallet. Swap apps need this before they can trade for you.
  • Unlimited approval: The app may move far more than is needed for the one operation, often your entire balance of that token, now and in the future. Many apps ask for this to save you a second transaction. If your wallet offers it, rather enter only the amount you need right now.
  • Revoke: You can remove an approval later. That costs a small network fee. The app simply asks again the next time you use it.

How to revoke an approval

  1. In Vow.id, open the “Security” area and select the entry.
  2. Tap “Review & revoke”. The window shows the network, the token, the authorised address and the change “Set to zero / remove”.
  3. Confirm the transaction in your wallet. It costs a small fee in BNB.
  4. Wait for the green message “Permission revoked and confirmed on-chain”. Only then is the revocation valid.
  5. Repeat this for each entry individually. Ticking several boxes does not replace the individual confirmations in the wallet.

The list does not always update straight away. What to do then is described under “When something gets stuck”.

The wallet scan in Vow.id (“Security” area) shows you your existing approvals. It does not find everything: some signatures exist only off the blockchain and show up in no scan. A revocation also does not bring back anything that is already gone. It is one building block, not all-round protection.

The most expensive beginner mistakes

  • Entering or photographing the seed phrase. Any page that asks for it is a scam, even if it looks genuine.
  • Choosing the wrong network. For Vow.id always BNB Smart Chain (BSC, BEP20).
  • Typing an address instead of copying it. One wrong character and the money is gone. After pasting, compare the first and last four characters.
  • Confirming a transaction without reading it. A signature for logging in is harmless. A transaction or an “approval” can move funds. Only confirm what you understand.
  • Responding to “support” in chats. Real support does not contact you first and never asks for codes or words.
  • Tapping several times because it hangs. That is how double payments happen. Check first, then try again.

Terms in one sentence

Wallet
The app you use to identify yourself and manage crypto assets.
Seed phrase
12 words that restore your wallet on any device. The master key.
Address
The public number of your wallet, begins with 0x. Anyone may know it.
Network
The blockchain something sits on. For Vow.id, the BNB Smart Chain.
BNB
The currency in which fees are paid on the BNB Smart Chain.
Gas
Another word for the network fee of a transaction.
Token
An asset on a blockchain, for example VOW, v$ or USDT.
Signature
A free signature that proves the wallet belongs to you.
Approval
Permission for an app to move tokens out of your wallet.
Transaction
An operation on the blockchain. Costs a fee and cannot be undone.
Stablecoin
A token pegged to the dollar, such as USDT or USDC.
BSC
Short for BNB Smart Chain, the network Vow.id uses. Also called “BEP20” in some apps.
KYC
Identity check with an ID document, required by purchase services and exchanges.
DEX
Decentralised exchange such as PancakeSwap, where you swap tokens straight from your wallet.
2FA
Two-step sign-in: in addition to the wallet, a code from an authenticator app.
T&Cs
Vow.id's terms and conditions, called “Terms and conditions” on the site.
MR
Member Rewards. A free, non-transferable entry in a smart contract, permanently tied to your wallet.
Pending MR
Shown as “Pending” in the app. A proposal, not yet an approved reward. Has no monetary value and can be rejected.
Approved MR
Shown as “Active” in the app. Approved by three independent verifiers. Redeemable for 60 days, after which they expire.
Exercising
The official term for redeeming: converting approved MR into v$ in return for a voluntary VOW contribution.
v$
Voucher Dollar. A transferable right to propose or accept a $1 discount. Not money and not redeemable for money.
VOW
The token you contribute when redeeming MR.
Handle
Your name at Vow.id, and at the same time your receipt address name@vow.id.
Vault
Your data area in Vow.id for contact details, addresses and settings.
Affiliate tree
The referral directory on the blockchain. Every member has a fixed place in it.
Verifier
Independent reviewers who approve or challenge MR proposals (VV and QVV).
Plaid
The service provider through which Premium members connect their bank account read-only.

What you should know before you put money in

Signing up and collecting MR cost nothing. You only put money in when you buy Premium or redeem MR with VOW.

  • Nobody guarantees that a proposal will be approved, that a merchant will accept v$ or that v$ will keep a particular value.
  • VOW that you contribute when redeeming is gone for good. It is not paid back.
  • VOW and v$ are crypto assets. Their price can fall sharply, up to a total loss.
  • Transactions on the blockchain cannot be undone.
  • Vow.id does not hold funds in custody and gives no investment advice. Neither does this page.
